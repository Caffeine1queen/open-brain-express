## The problem

You watch a video that changes how you think about something. You read an
article worth remembering. An idea arrives in the car. Six months later, all of
it is gone — you have a vague sense you knew something once, and no way back to
it.

Notes apps don't fix this. They're good at storing things and bad at giving them
back. You have to remember what you called it, and which folder it went in, to
ever find it again.

## What you're building

**A private memory that any AI can read.**

You save things into it — typed notes, spoken thoughts, YouTube videos, PDFs,
articles. It reads each one, works out what it's about, and files it. Then it
does something notes apps don't: it looks for anything else you've saved that's
*related*, and quietly links them together.

Over time it becomes a web rather than a pile.

Three things make it different from an app you'd download:

**It understands meaning, not just words — but it still catches the words that
matter.** Search "how do I get new clients" and it finds the note you wrote
about customer acquisition, even though you never used that phrase. And if you
search for an exact name, an account number, or an unusual term, it catches
that too — the two ways of searching work together, not instead of each other.

**It connects things on its own.** Save a thought today about pricing, and it
links itself to a video you saved in March about the same idea. You don't tag
anything. You'll find things you'd completely forgotten.

**It belongs to you.** The database is in your account, on your card. Nobody can
raise the price, change the terms, or shut it down. You can export everything,
any time. And because it uses an open standard, if you switch away from Claude
one day, you point the new AI at the same brain.

## What happens when you save something

You paste a YouTube link. About thirty seconds later there's a summary of what
was actually *said* in that video, tagged, categorised, and already connected to
two other things you saved months ago on the same theme. The full transcript
stays behind it too, so a detail the summary didn't mention is still there to
be found.

You didn't do any of that. That's the whole point.

## How to use it

**Feed it, without thinking about it.** This is the only habit that matters.
When you finish something worth remembering — a video, an article, a
conversation, an idea in the shower — put it in. Ten seconds. Install it on your
phone so it's one tap.

The brain is worth exactly what you put in. Nothing else you do with it matters
as much.

**Then ask Claude about yourself.** Once it's connected, Claude can read
everything you've saved. Things worth asking:

> **"What have I been thinking about lately?"**
> It reads your recent captures and tells you — often spotting a theme you
> hadn't noticed.

> **"Search my brain for [something], and tell me what connects to it."**
> This is where the links pay off. Ideas resurface that you'd forgotten you had.

> **"I'm about to write about X. What do I already know about it?"**
> Instead of starting from a blank page, you start from your own accumulated
> thinking.

> **"Based on everything in here, what am I avoiding?"**
> Ask this one when it's a few months full. It's uncomfortable.

**Give it a month before you judge it.** With ten things in it, it's
underwhelming. With two hundred, it starts telling you things about yourself.
The value curve is slow and then sharp.

## What it costs

The storage and hosting are free. The only paid part is a few dollars of AI
credit that does the reading and tagging — most people spend well under a dollar
a month. The app shows you exactly what you've spent, so you never have to take
anyone's word for it.

## Why bother

Every AI you talk to starts from zero. It knows the whole internet and nothing
about you.

This is the part it's missing: what *you* found worth keeping. Feed it for a
year and you'll have something no AI company can sell you, because it's made of
your own attention.
